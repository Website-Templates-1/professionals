Almost every small business runs on spreadsheets, and for good reason. They are
flexible, familiar, and already on everyone's screen. The trouble is that a
spreadsheet rarely announces when it has become the wrong tool. It just gets
slower, more fragile, and quietly more expensive until one bad afternoon of lost
data makes the problem obvious. Here are the signs to watch for, and what to do
before that afternoon arrives.

## First, spreadsheets are not the enemy

To be clear: this is not an argument against spreadsheets. For modelling numbers,
one-off analysis, or small, stable lists, they are hard to beat, and we happily
build [websites and tools powered by Google Sheets](/blog/automate-repetitive-tasks-with-google-sheets)
when a spreadsheet is the right engine. The question is not "are spreadsheets
bad?" It is "has this particular spreadsheet quietly grown into something it was
never designed to be?"

## The seven signs

### 1. Several people fight over the same file

If two people open the file and overwrite each other's work, or you coordinate
who is "allowed in" right now, the spreadsheet is being used as a shared system it
was never built to be. Version conflicts and lost edits are a reliable early
warning.

### 2. There's a "real version" nobody may touch

When one master file has become so critical that only one person is trusted to
edit it, and everyone else works from copies, you have a single point of failure
with no safeguards. That file is doing the job of a database, without any of a
database's protection.

### 3. Errors keep creeping in

A mistyped formula, a row deleted by accident, a number pasted into the wrong
column, these are not carelessness, they are what happens when important logic
lives in cells anyone can overwrite. If small errors keep causing real problems,
the tool is working against you.

### 4. You copy data between systems by hand

If someone regularly exports from one place and pastes into the spreadsheet, or
retypes the same figures into two systems, that manual bridge is slow and
error-prone. This is often the easiest thing to fix, and where
[automation](/blog/what-is-business-process-automation) pays back fastest.

### 5. The file is slow or enormous

Spreadsheets bog down as they grow. If it takes seconds to open, lags when you
scroll, or has tens of thousands of rows, you are past the size a spreadsheet
handles comfortably. Structured data at that scale belongs in a proper database.

### 6. You rebuild the same report every period

If someone recreates the same weekly or monthly report by hand, pulling numbers,
arranging a template, formatting a summary, that repetition is a clear signal.
Recurring, rule-based work is exactly what should run on its own.

### 7. It holds data it shouldn't

Customer details, payments, or anything sensitive sitting in a shared spreadsheet
is a real risk. Spreadsheets lack the access controls, audit trails and security a
proper system provides. Sensitive or transactional data is a sign you have
outgrown the tool, not a limit to work around.

## A quick self-check

| If this is true... | ...you probably need |
| --- | --- |
| One painful task is manual and repetitive | Targeted **automation** around the sheet |
| Data is copied between tools by hand | **Integrations** so systems share data |
| Multiple people need controlled, shared access | A **web application** with real logins |
| Data is large, sensitive or transactional | A **proper database and backend** |

Most businesses recognise more than one row. That is normal, and it points to the
order in which to fix things.

## What to do about it (without overbuilding)

Outgrowing spreadsheets does not mean ripping everything out and commissioning a
big platform. The opposite, usually. The smart path is incremental:

- **Automate the worst task first.** Often the fastest relief is automating the
  single most painful, repetitive job, the manual report, the copy-paste, the
  reminders, while the spreadsheet stays in place. If that is where you are, our
  overview of [business process automation](/blog/what-is-business-process-automation)
  and our [spreadsheet automation](/spreadsheet-automation) service are built for
  exactly this. This is the fix when the *tasks around* the sheet hurt, rather
  than the sheet itself.
- **Connect your tools.** If the pain is data living in two places, integrations
  can keep them in sync so nobody retypes anything.
- **Move to a real application when access and scale demand it.** When many people
  need controlled, shared access, or the data has outgrown a file, a
  [web application](/web-application-development) with a proper database gives you
  logins, validation and reliability a spreadsheet cannot.

The signal for a full custom build is the same "almost fits" tax we describe in
our guide on [custom software vs off-the-shelf](/blog/custom-software-vs-off-the-shelf):
when you are paying people to compensate for the tool, you are already paying for
software, just in wasted hours.

most painful part, run the new solution alongside the spreadsheet until you trust
