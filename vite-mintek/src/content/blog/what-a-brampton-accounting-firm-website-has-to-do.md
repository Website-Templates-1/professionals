A Brampton accounting firm's website has one job before it has a design. A person who needs a CPA, a bookkeeper, or a tax filing has to see that this firm does that work, in this city, and how to start.

That is a narrower job than a generic small-business site. The visitor is often handing over financial information. The page has to feel specific and current. A slider and a stock photo of a handshake do not do that.

This is what that site has to do, and what we actually built for one Brampton firm.

## Name the work, the people, and the place

The homepage should say what the firm files and who it files for. Personal tax, corporate tax, bookkeeping, and advisory are different conversations. If the firm does three of those, the site should say three, in the firm's own words. If it does not do one of them, the site should not imply that it does.

The city belongs on the page when the office is there. A Brampton firm with a Brampton address can say Brampton once, in the copy a client reads, and repeat the same name, address, and phone that appear on the Google Business Profile. Stuffing the city into every heading does not make the firm easier to hire.

The same standard applies to the people. A visitor wants to know who will be on the other side of the consultation. A logo and a contact form leave that blank.

## Give them one way to start

Most people looking for an accountant are not ready to upload a shoebox of receipts on the first visit. They are ready to ask whether the firm can take them on.

The useful next step is a consultation, named as a consultation, wired to whatever the firm actually answers: a booking link, a phone number, or a short form. "Contact us" can hide that. A twelve-field form can hide it too, if the firm really starts with a phone call.

On the Aloe site, that path is a free-consultation booking plus clear calls to action. The detail of the build is in the [Aloe Accounting case study](/case-studies/aloe-accounting).

## Keep proof honest

Reviews matter to a person choosing an accountant. They do not belong on the site as invented star ratings, copied Google text the firm has no right to reuse, or an AggregateRating block the firm made up. A dashboard that lets the owner view and reply to the reviews Google already has is a different thing. That is what Aloe's review screen does, including AI-assisted reply drafts. The owner still decides what gets sent.

Lighthouse scores are a lab check, not a ranking. The published case study records mobile scores of SEO 100, Best Practices 100, Accessibility 93, and Performance 96. Those numbers say the page passed that tool's checks on the run we recorded. They do not say where the firm shows up in Google.

## Publish only what the firm will stand behind

A CPA site that posts tax commentary has to be right. A blog nobody at the firm reads is how a wrong sentence gets the firm's name on it.

Aloe's setup is built around that constraint. The owner keeps a queue of topics. One draft is generated each week. She edits it and approves it. Nothing goes live before that approval. There is no agency retainer sitting between the firm and the next article. The point of the workflow is that the firm stays in charge of what it says.

A firm that will not review a draft should not buy the workflow. A page of services and a consultation link is a complete website. The weekly draft is for a practice that wants a publishing habit and will spend the time to keep it accurate.

That workflow is [business automation](/business-automation) attached to a marketing site, not a feature every homepage needs on day one.

## What we would leave off

A second office the firm does not have. A list of services copied from a competitor. A chat widget that promises an instant answer about someone's tax situation. A page of city names — Mississauga, Vaughan, Toronto — with the same paragraph repeated under each.

If the firm serves clients across the GTA from a Brampton office, one sentence can say that. The sentence has to be true. It does not need a doorway page per city.

We would also leave off any score presented as a guarantee. A fast page and a clear consultation are the job. A Lighthouse medal in a sales proposal is not.
